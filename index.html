A little more about me.

I grew up in a small town in Rajasthan, India. I spent the first eighteen years of my life in the same school, with the same faces, on the same streets. I spent a lot of that time simply watching things.

NIFT is where I found photography and design. I fell in love with the process, from the first idea to the final image or layout. It was the first time I could say something honest without needing words. I have always carried a lot inside, and photography and design gave it a place to go.

During COVID I had no studio and no equipment. So I built my own lights and used whatever I found around the house to make photographs. I did not stop. That is how I approach things I care about, and it shaped how I solve problems today. If I want a certain look and lack the right tool or skill, I learn it fast and find an unconventional way to get there. The method might be strange, but the result holds up. That instinct carries into my graphic design work too: I pick up new software and techniques quickly, and I keep pushing a layout or a visual until it feels right.

After NIFT I received offers from schools I had dreamed about for years: RCA, Speos, Parsons, Leeds. I chose Grenoble instead, and it became one of the best decisions I have made. That city felt like home faster than I expected. Now I live in Paris, in my second year, still finding my way.

Visuals, books, stories, and concepts all connect for me. One feeds the next. A word becomes an image. An image becomes an idea. That connection is what draws me toward creative and strategic roles across graphic design, brand, communication, and visual storytelling, not one fixed path.

Outside work, I work out to keep my mind clear. I write in my journal most days. I enjoy quiet evenings, though weekends usually pull me out into the world. I read often, and I love fantasy worlds, though I sometimes avoid stories that hit too close to real emotion.

There is more to me than usually comes through. It does not always show up in words, but it finds its way into everything I make.
